COPILOT WORKSPACES · TEAM PLAYBOOK

Your Notebooks are now Workspaces. Here's how to set one up right.

A Workspace is where your team and Copilot work on the same project, with the same files and the same context. Set it up well once and nobody has to re-explain the project to Copilot again.

What changed

Notebooks grew up and got roommates

Copilot Notebooks were great for one person collecting files and asking questions about them. Workspaces keep all of that and make it a team thing. Microsoft describes a Workspace as a shared place where you and your team keep project information and files together and work with Copilot on an ongoing workstream.

  • Automatic

    Your existing notebooks become workspaces. Everything you added or created stays, and so does any sharing you'd set up. If it wasn't shared, it stays private.

  • Frontier

    Live cursors and editing across documents at the same time, plus agents working alongside you.

  • Rolling out

    Creating and editing Word, Excel and PowerPoint files from the workspace chat. Microsoft is targeting worldwide availability in January.

Why it matters

Most of the work Copilot helps with is team work, but until now the context lived in one person's chat. A Workspace puts the context where the whole team, and the agents helping them, can use it.

Workspace or Project?

Your desk vs. the team's project room

Copilot now has two places to keep context, and they're easy to mix up. Think of a Project as your desk. It's personal and holds your chats, tasks, files and results, and it works with Chat and Cowork. A Workspace is the team's project room, with the whiteboard everyone can see.

ProjectWorkspace
Who it's forJust youYour team
Best forYour own tasks, drafts and researchA shared workstream with a shared goal
Works withChat and CoworkThe Copilot app, and Teams for ongoing conversation
Moving workDraft in your Project, then move the file into the Workspace when it's ready to share.

My rule of thumb

If you'd say "I'm working on it," it's a Project. If you'd say "we're working on it," it's a Workspace. One Workspace per workstream, not one per team. A team with five projects gets five Workspaces.

Set one up

Five steps to a Workspace people actually use

  1. Pick one workstream with a finish line

    "Q1 product launch" works. "Marketing stuff" doesn't. A clear goal gives Copilot something to aim at, and gives you a natural point to archive it.

  2. Name it so search finds it

    Use the format Team · Workstream · Timeframe, like Cafe Events · Spring Open Mic Series · Mar–May. You can rename it later from the arrow next to the name.

  3. Add references on purpose

    Add what the work actually depends on: the brief, the plan, last year's version, the key meeting recap. Five great files beat fifty random ones, because every file you add is something Copilot will treat as true.

  4. Write the team brief first

    Before anyone asks Copilot anything, add a one-page brief with the goal, who owns what, terms you use and what "done" looks like. It's the single biggest quality boost. Template below.

  5. Invite the team, then run a kickoff prompt together

    Use Invite in the upper-right corner. Then ask Copilot, as a group, to summarize the plan and list open questions. If the summary's wrong, fix your references now, not three weeks in.

Why the brief matters

Copilot is only as good as the context you give it. A Workspace without a brief is like a new hire on day one with a pile of files and no manager. They'll read everything and still guess at what matters.

What to leave out

Old drafts, files with outdated numbers, and anything most of the team shouldn't see. Your normal Microsoft 365 permissions and compliance controls still apply, but a shared space is the wrong place for sensitive files that only one person needs.

The team brief

Copy this into your first page

Fill in the brackets, save it as the first page in your Workspace, and tell your team it's the source of truth.

TEAM BRIEF: [Workstream name]

GOAL
[One sentence. What will be true when we're done?]

DEADLINE / FINISH LINE
[Date, or the event that ends this work]

WHO OWNS WHAT
- [Name]: [area they own and approve]
- [Name]: [area]
- Copilot / agents: [what they can draft; a person reviews before anything ships]

WORDS WE USE
- [Term]: [what we mean by it]
- [Term]: [what we mean by it]

SOURCES OF TRUTH
- [File]: [what it's the source of truth for]
- If two files disagree, [file] wins.

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
- Tone: [e.g. friendly, plain language, no jargon]
- Format: [e.g. one-page summaries, tables for numbers]
- Never: [e.g. share pricing before it's approved]

OPEN QUESTIONS
- [Question] (owner: [name])

Make it work for Copilot too

The "Sources of truth" and "Never" sections do the heavy lifting. They tell Copilot which file wins when two disagree and what it should never assume. People skip those, and they're the reason answers go sideways.

Working with agents

Treat agents like a teammate who drafts

This is the part that changes how teams work. In a Workspace, agents can draft part of a document for a person to review, and act on a comment someone leaves. Autopilot can take on a task and check in with you before anything is final.

  • Frontier

    Agents drafting sections, acting on comments, and Autopilot taking tasks inside a Workspace.

Three ground rules I'd set on day one

  1. Every agent draft has a human owner. Put the reviewer's name in the brief next to what the agent drafts.
  2. Ask in comments, not side chats. A comment on the doc keeps the request, the context and the result together where the whole team can see them.
  3. Nothing leaves the Workspace unreviewed. Agents can move fast inside it. Anything sent to customers, leadership or the public gets a person's eyes first.

Why it matters

The risk with agents isn't bad drafts. It's drafts nobody owns. Writing ownership into the brief means everyone knows who checks what, including the agents.

Starter kit

See it filled in, then steal the prompts

Here's a worked example from April's Acoustic Cafe, the fictional cafe and music shop I use in SkillUp. The events team is launching a spring open mic series.

Example workspace

Cafe Events · Spring Open Mic Series · Mar–May

Goal
Run six open mic nights with at least 12 performers each, and grow the mailing list by 300.
People
Maya (events, approves the schedule), Theo (marketing, approves posts), Jules (store manager, approves budget).
References
Last year's open mic recap, the 2027 events budget, the performer sign-up form responses, the venue capacity and safety notes.
Agents
Drafts social posts and performer emails. Theo reviews everything public.

Five prompts to run in your first week

1. Kickoff: "Using the team brief and the references in this workspace, summarize our plan in five bullets and list the open questions we haven't answered."

2. Gaps: "What information would you need to do this work well that isn't in this workspace yet?"

3. Owners: "Turn the plan into a task list with an owner and due date for each item, using the owners in the team brief. Flag anything without an owner."

4. Draft: "Draft [the first deliverable] for [owner] to review. Follow 'What good looks like' in the brief and list any assumptions you made."

5. Weekly check-in: "What changed in this workspace this week, what's at risk, and what decision do we need from someone?"

The prompt I'd never skip

Prompt 2. Asking Copilot what's missing is the fastest way to find the file you forgot to add or the decision nobody's made yet.

Before you start

Rollout notes for your team

  • It's rolling out in phases. Availability varies by organization, so what you see may not match a screenshot yet. Your admin can check the Microsoft 365 Message center.
  • Start in the Copilot app. The new team features show up there first. Workspaces in OneNote get them over time.
  • Nothing is lost. Microsoft says no content loss is expected, and existing links keep working with redirects where an address changes.
  • Your controls still apply. Core Microsoft 365 security, compliance and governance controls continue to apply, so there's no new permission model to learn.

For admins

No immediate action is required to keep existing content available. The useful work is people work: pick one or two pilot teams, give them the brief template, and see what they learn in the first month.
